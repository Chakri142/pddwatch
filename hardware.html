Deep Research: The Miniaturization Challenge

As a CSE student jumping into ECE, you will notice that prototype boards (like the GPS and ESP32 listed above) are heavily bulky. To fit these into a child's smartwatch, you must master Miniaturization.

1. The Micro-Soldering Action

Header pins are too tall for a watch. You must desolder the black pins from your modules and use 0.1mm Enamelled Magnet Wire. Here is the action shot of fusing two components directly pad-to-pad to save 12mm of height.

2. The Final Assembled Sub-Stack

This is the ultimate layout blueprint. It shows the transparent casing revealing the "Stack" methodology: LiPo Battery on the bottom, Custom merged Sensor/Brain PCB in the middle, and the LCD Screen floating on top, with the camera facing strictly UPWARD past the wrist.

CSE to ECE Tip: Transition to Custom Bare-Die Design

For mass production, you will abandon the 'Breakout Boards' entirely. You will buy the raw, naked ESP32-PICO chip ($1.50) and the raw MAX30205 sensor ($2.00) and design a single, custom printed 4-layer circular PCB. This is how Apple and Garmin fit everything inside a watch! The prototype blueprint provides the logic, but the Custom PCB provides the form factor.
